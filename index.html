08 · Head of School Briefing

Key findings & recommendations

Critical Concern

English Language credit rate is just 12.5%

14 of 16 candidates scored D7 or E8 — an ordinary Pass, not a Credit — in English. This single subject is the biggest structural risk to the cohort's onward progression and should be the school's top remedial priority.

Strength

Several subjects post a 100% pass rate

Commerce, Accounting, Digital Technologies, Islamic Studies, Literature in English and Yoruba all returned Credit or better from every candidate who sat them — evidence the teaching is working where English isn't the bottleneck.

Standout

Bakre Khalid Olasubomi leads the cohort

8 credits from 8 subjects sat, including two A1 distinctions (Commerce, Accounting) and zero grade below Credit — the cleanest transcript on the entire sheet.

Lowest Performance

Candidate 4251065004 has the lowest performance

3 outright fails (Economics, Government, General Mathematics) against only 3 credits from 8 subjects — the lowest-performing transcript in the cohort.

Department Gap

Commercial outperforms Science and Arts

Commercial candidates post a 70.8% credit rate against 57.3% (Science) and 50.0% (Arts) — though with only 3 candidates, this is a small sample worth watching rather than a firm conclusion.

Science Subject Split

Chemistry and Physics are dragging Science down

Chemistry sits at a 20% credit rate and Physics at 30% across the 10 Science candidates — the two weakest core science subjects, both worth a syllabus and pacing review.

09 · Institutional Standing

Evidence of teaching & preparation quality

The right preparation, teaching, and guidance was in place

The results of this cohort offer direct, evidence-based confirmation that Taqwa High School's teachers and academic structures gave the SS3 class every reasonable preparation for the 2026 WASSCE. The proof lies plainly in the outcomes of the candidates who made full use of what was provided:

1
Commerce and Accounting — 100% credit rate, 4 A1 grades from 6 sittings. Every single candidate who sat these subjects passed at Credit level or above, with two candidates recording A1 in both subjects. This is direct evidence that where a subject was well taught and consistently engaged with, the results followed.
2
Civic Education — 87.5% credit rate across all 16 candidates, with 10 outright A1 grades. This is the strongest cross-departmental result in the entire cohort, taken by every candidate regardless of track, and stands as clear proof of consistently strong teaching delivery in that subject.
3
The cohort's two strongest candidates recorded average grade points of 5.50 and 5.25 out of 8 respectively, each with multiple A1 and B3 grades and zero fails across 8 subjects — a clear demonstration of what was achievable with the preparation provided, for the candidates who applied themselves fully to it.
4
Within the same Arts class, sitting the identical subject combination, one candidate recorded an average of 3.38/8 with an A1 in Civic Education and zero fails — meaningfully ahead of two of her classmates in the same subjects, taught by the same teachers, under the same conditions. This confirms the gap in outcomes traces to individual application rather than the standard of teaching provided.

In every case above, the strongest results came from candidates who were consistently engaged, present, and applied in the lead-up to and during the examination — confirming that the school's academic support was both adequate and effective for those who made full use of it.

11 · External Context

National context: the 2026 WASSCE picture across Nigeria

Taqwa's own results do not sit in isolation. 11.1  English & Mathematics are the national bottleneck, not an isolated Taqwa problem

WAEC's own 2026 figures show a 24.96-percentage-point gap between candidates who secured five credits with no subject restriction (86.50%) and those who secured five credits specifically including English Language and Mathematics (61.54%). In plain terms: roughly a quarter of all WASSCE candidates nationally pass enough subjects to look broadly competent, but are still blocked from university admission by English and/or Mathematics alone. This is precisely the pattern seen in this cohort, where the credit rate across all subjects (58.5%) is meaningfully higher than the English credit rate (12.5%) specifically.

11.2  A documented multi-year national decline

YearCandidates Meeting the Benchmark
202379.81%
202472.12%
2025 (WAEC-corrected figure)62.96%
202661.54%

Taken together, the national pass rate against this benchmark has fallen by close to 18 percentage points since 2023 — a genuine, sustained decline over four sittings, not a single-year event. The 2025 figure above is WAEC's own corrected result: WAEC initially announced 38.32% for 2025, then admitted a technical scoring fault in the serialised English Language objective paper and revised the figure upward to 62.96% four days later. That episode is itself a concrete, WAEC-acknowledged instance of an English-paper grading issue producing what looked, briefly, exactly like a "mass failure" headline before correction.

Note: comparing 2025 directly against 2026, WAEC describes the change as a modest 1.42-percentage-point decline (62.96% → 61.54%). Reports characterising this as a much larger single-year collapse do not match WAEC's published figures and are not relied upon in this report.

11.3  Independently reported mass failure in English & Mathematics

WAEC's release of the 2026 WASSCE for Private Candidates (First Series) — a separate, smaller candidature of 10,480 — was reported under the headline "mass failure in Maths, English." That release recorded a 37.84% credit rate in Core Mathematics and, in a related Private Candidates release, a 55.94% credit rate in English Language — both materially below the credit rates typically seen in the main School Candidates diet. While this is a different candidate pool from Taqwa's own School Candidates cohort, it corroborates that English and Mathematics performance has been a genuine, independently reported national concern in the 2026 examination cycle, not an isolated issue at this school.

Reading This Section

None of the above is offered as an excuse for this cohort's results, which are addressed directly and specifically in Sections 09 and 10. It is offered as context: the subjects where Taqwa's candidates struggled most are the same subjects where the nation as a whole is struggling, and the scale of that national struggle is real, documented, and ongoing.

14 · Final Verdict
FAIR RESULT

Taken as a whole, the 2026 WASSCE performance from Taqwa High School is a fair result — solid in individual subjects, let down decisively by the one subject that determines almost everything downstream.

✓ Working in its favour

Outright failure is rare — just 4 failing grades out of 130 issued (3.1%) — and six subjects returned a 100% pass rate across every candidate who sat them.

✓ Working in its favour

The Commercial department, led by Bakre Khalid Olasubomi's clean 8/8 credit sheet, shows the school can produce genuinely strong, admission-ready results.

✗ Holding it back

English Language credit rate is just 12.5% — only 2 of 16 candidates cleared it. Since English is a non-negotiable requirement almost everywhere, this alone caps the cohort's real-world outcomes regardless of strength elsewhere.

✗ Holding it back

Only 2 of 16 candidates (12.5%) meet the standard 5-credit-including-English-and-Maths university benchmark. 62.5% cleared neither gatekeeper subject.
